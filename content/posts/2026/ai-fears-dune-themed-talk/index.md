I always look forward to Texas Linux Fest each year and the event just wrapped up in Austin.
It's such a great event because you can _really connect_ with people who are doing some really interesting work and are willing to tell you all about it.

I submitted a talk for this year's conference about engineering fears and AI with a Dune theme.
The [talk abstract] was fun to write, but building the presentation itself was extremely challenging.
How do you make a talk about fears of the unknown while also being upbeat and finding ways to keep the audience awake?

The audience asked some great questions at the end about several important topics:

* How do junior engineers level up in this new world?
* Are there areas where AI should and should not be used?
* What about the people who are completely and adimantly against anything AI-related in their work?

For all of these questions, I strongly believe that we will adapt lots of the things we've always done before.
Many of the principles still apply but they might look a little different.

I did talk a bit about stoicism and I found that few in the audience were familiar with the ideals.
If you want to read more about that, I highly recommend [_The Practicing Stoic_] by Ward Farnsworth.
The book walks you through the simplest aspects of stoicism up to the more complex ones with tons of quotes from famous stoic thinkers along the way.
It's amazing to see so many things written in the second century that apply so well in 2026.

[_The Practicing Stoic_]: https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/37886498-the-practicing-stoic

If you haven't experienced the world of Dune, I highly recommend the new series of movies.
